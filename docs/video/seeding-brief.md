# Seeding brief — demo account data

Run this before recording. It costs your own OpenAI spend and roughly an hour, and it
uses no Higgsfield credits. Everything in the shot list depends on it.

The goal is a believable improvement story on `/progress`: a trend that rises, with a
dip in the middle. Perfect scores are not the target and would actively hurt the video.

---

## Before you start: fix the profile

`My Profile` currently holds a real senior job description ("Integration Leader, Head
of Fund Control"). That would appear on camera in shots 3 and 8.

Replace the target role with something generic. **Suggested: "Operations Analyst".**

A note on that choice. The obvious option is "Graduate Analyst", since graduates are
the audience. I would avoid it: you would have to invent early-career stories and act
them, and hesitant delivery shows badly on camera. "Operations Analyst" is generic
enough for any viewer to project onto, and close enough to your own background that
you can speak from real experience and sound natural. Naturalness matters more here
than exact audience mirroring, because the viewer is watching the *interface*, not
your CV.

Keep the same role for all five sessions. The trend chart groups by role.

## The score arc

Five sessions, in this order. The recalibrated scorer now awards the top of the range
honestly, so these are achievable without exaggeration.

| Session | Target | How to deliver |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | **6** | Real examples, but stay general. Do not quantify anything. End answers on what happened, with no reflection. |
| S2 | **7** | Same stories, add one specific number per answer. |
| S3 | **5** | **The deliberate dip.** See below. |
| S4 | **7** | Back to S2 quality, different examples. |
| S5 | **8** | Specific situation, clear actions in the first person, a quantified result, and one line of genuine reflection. |

**How to produce the dip in S3 without it looking sabotaged:** answer in generalities.
Talk about what "we" did rather than what you did, describe the situation at length and
the action briefly, and finish without a result. That is exactly what an average real
answer looks like, and it will land at 5 or 6 honestly. Do not mumble, go silent, or
give a non-answer — that scores 0–2 and puts an ugly outlier on the chart.

---

## What actually moves the score

From the scoring bands the marker now uses, so you can steer deliberately:

- **A specific situation** rather than a general claim. "In my last role we handled
  escalations" is a 5. "A supplier missed a delivery deadline three days before month
  end" is on the way to a 7.
- **"I", not "we".** The marker cannot score a team.
- **A number in the result.** A percentage, a time saving, a volume, an error rate.
  This is the single biggest lever between 6 and 8.
- **One line of reflection** at the end — what you would do differently. This is what
  separates 8 from 9.

Aim for **90 seconds to two minutes** per answer. Running long is the most common way
strong content scores badly, and the length feedback will show it.

## Story bank

Five stories, so no session reuses one. Adapt whichever fits the competency you are
asked about — the questions are generated, so you will not know them in advance.

1. **A deadline that moved.** Two pieces of work collided; you chose what to protect and
   told someone early.
2. **A process you improved** that nobody asked you to touch.
3. **A disagreement with a colleague** where they turned out to have a fair point.
4. **Something that went wrong** and what you changed afterwards so it could not recur.
5. **Explaining something technical** to someone who needed to act on it, not understand it.

For S5, use whichever of these you can attach a real number to.

## Assessment centre run

One complete run, all three stages, after the five practice sessions. Shots 6 and 7
need a real case study on screen and a real report.

- Same role, so it sits consistently alongside the practice history.
- **Stage 1:** write a genuine structured answer. Lead with the recommendation, use two
  exhibits, name a risk. It does not need to be brilliant, but it must be serious — the
  case study score appears in shot 7.
- **Stage 2:** five questions, same standard as S5.
- **Stage 3:** a real three-minute presentation.

Expect roughly 45–60 minutes.

## Practical notes

**All five will be dated today.** The trajectory chart plots points as S1…S5 rather than
by date, so a same-day run reads fine on the chart. Only the session history list shows
dates, and it is not the focus of shot 8. If you would rather spread them over two or
three days, the trend looks marginally more natural, but it is not worth delaying the
recording for.

**Do not delete the existing 1/10 session until the new ones exist** — if something goes
wrong you will still have a working account. Delete it afterwards so it does not appear
in the history list or drag the average.

**Check `/progress` before you record.** You are looking for: latest score 7 or 8, a
visible upward line with one dip, category averages that are not zero, and a readiness
signal that is not "Weak". If any of those are wrong, run one more session rather than
recording around it.

# Corporate demo — script and shot list (.co.uk)

**Target length:** 70 seconds
**Aspect:** 16:9 only. No 9:16 cut needed — this is not an ad, it sits on `/for-business`
and goes into university and employer conversations.
**Voice:** British English. Same voice as the candidate demo, read a notch more formally.
**Audience:** university careers services, and employers running graduate recruitment.

This video has a completely different job from the candidate one. The candidate demo
sells relief. This one sells **capacity** and **defensibility** — the two things a
careers lead is actually judged on.

---

## Why this is not the candidate script with different words

A careers adviser does not have an interview on Thursday. Their problem is that four
hundred students need mock interviews and there are two of them. They cannot scale
themselves, so students who ask late get nothing.

An employer's problem is different again: they need every candidate assessed on the
same basis, and to be able to show that if challenged.

So the emotional beat here is not fear, it is **the thing you had to say no to**. That
is a more dignified pitch and it is closer to true.

The candidate video opens with no interface for eight seconds. This one opens with a
number, because this audience is persuaded by scale rather than by feeling.

## Voiceover script

> **(0–4s)**
> "A careers team of three. Eleven hundred final-year students."
>
> **(4–9s)**
> "You can't give them all a mock interview. So the ones who ask late don't get one."
>
> **(9–17s)**
> "This is a way to give every one of them the same thing. You set up an assessment
> once, and issue it."
>
> **(17–26s)**
> "You choose the format. Competency, strengths, technical. Your own framework if you
> have one, your own instructions if you need them."
>
> **(26–35s)**
> "Every student gets the identical brief, on their own time, as many attempts as you
> allow."
>
> **(35–45s)**
> "And it comes back scored on the same basis. Not a note in someone's diary. Actual
> comparable results."
>
> **(45–54s)**
> "With the evidence behind every score, so you can defend it, and so the student can
> learn from it."
>
> **(54–62s)**
> "Which leaves your team doing the part software can't do. Talking to the student in
> front of them."
>
> **(62–70s)**
> "AI Career Mentor. Interview practice, at the scale you actually need."

**Word count:** ~175 across 70 seconds. Slightly denser than the candidate script,
because this audience will not mind information and does mind having its time wasted.

---

## Voice direction

- Same British voice as the candidate demo, for brand consistency.
- **More level, less warmth.** This is a peer-to-peer read: someone who understands the
  operational problem, not someone selling relief.
- The opening two numbers should be stated plainly, with a beat between them. "A careers
  team of three." *(beat)* "Eleven hundred final-year students."
- Land firmly on "Actual comparable results" and "so you can defend it" — those are the
  two lines a careers lead is listening for.
- Do not soften the ending. "At the scale you actually need" should be a statement.

---

## The numbers in the opening

"Three" and "eleven hundred" are illustrative and deliberately unbranded. They are
plausible for a mid-size UK university careers service.

**Do not attribute them to a named institution**, and do not present them as research.
If you would rather avoid specific figures entirely, this alternative works and makes no
factual claim:

> "However big your careers team is, it isn't big enough for every final-year student
> who needs a mock interview."

Slightly weaker as an opening, entirely safe. Your call.

## Screen capture requirements

**Same settings as the candidate demo:** 1920×1080, 60fps, no browser chrome.

This one needs more setup than the candidate video, because shots 6 and 7 show
*multiple candidates*:

- A corporate/company account with a template configured
- **At least four candidate results**, with genuinely varied scores. A list where
  everyone scores 7 looks fabricated; a spread from 4 to 8 looks real.
- **Entirely fictional candidate names.** No real students, no real employees. This is
  the single most important constraint in this document — real candidate names and
  scores on a marketing video would be a data protection problem, not just poor taste.
- Fictional company name on the template if the branding is visible.

Shot 5 needs the candidate-side view, which may mean a second browser profile or an
incognito window signed in as a test candidate.

## AI generation notes (shots 1, 2, 8)

Consistency again matters: shots 1 and 8 are the same adviser on two different days.
Pin the character from the first approved still with `show_reference_elements`.

**Look:** a real UK university careers office, not a startup. Slightly institutional
furniture, noticeboards, natural light through a window. Warm but not glossy.

**Casting:** an adviser who reads as experienced — late 30s to 50s. This audience is
being asked to trust the product with their students, and a visibly senior person in
the frame does quiet work there.

**Avoid:** anything that looks like a stock "diverse team high-fiving" shot. This
audience is allergic to it, and it would undercut the credibility the rest of the video
is building.

## Build order

Record this in the **same session** as the candidate demo. The settings are identical
and the setup overlaps, so doing both at once saves an entire round of preparation.

1. Seed the corporate side: template plus four or more fictional candidate results.
2. Record the five capture shots.
3. Generate and iterate stills for shots 1, 2, 8.
4. Pin the adviser character, animate approved frames.
5. Voiceover, assemble, no reframe needed.

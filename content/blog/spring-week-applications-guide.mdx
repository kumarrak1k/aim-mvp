Spring weeks are the earliest formal doorway into competitive graduate careers: short insight programmes (two to five days, usually over the Easter break) run by banks, law firms, consultancies and accounting firms for first-year students (or second-years on four-year courses). They look minor on paper: some talks, some shadowing, a case exercise, a networking evening. They are not minor. At many firms a strong spring week performance leads to a fast-tracked or guaranteed summer internship interview, and summer internships are where most graduate offers are actually decided.

The practical consequence: for the most competitive industries, the funnel now starts in the autumn of first year, and many students find out after the deadlines have passed.

## The timeline nobody tells you about

Applications open from September and close between late October and January, with many firms recruiting on a rolling basis: they fill places as strong applications arrive, and a technically open deadline can be practically closed by December. Apply early. An application submitted in October is competing for a full quota; the same application in January may be competing for scraps.

The programmes themselves run March to April, and conversion interviews for summer internships often happen during or immediately after the week itself.

## What firms actually look for

Nobody expects a first-year to have experience. The sift is looking for three things:

- **Evidence you chose them on purpose.** Not prestige in general: this firm, this division. Why investment banking rather than asset management, why this firm rather than its nearest rival. Two specific reasons beat six generic ones. Our guide to [researching a company](/blog/how-to-research-a-company-before-an-interview) shows how to build them quickly.
- **Raw material.** Grades, yes, but also anything that shows drive with a result attached: a part-time job held for two years, a society you actually ran something in, a project with an outcome. School-era achievements still count at this stage.
- **Basic professional signal.** An application with no typos, submitted early, with a CV on one page ([format guide here](/blog/how-to-write-a-cv-uk)) clears a surprising share of the field on its own.

The written question that decides most applications is some version of why us, why this industry. Draft it per firm, name real things (a division, a deal or case that interested you, a person you spoke to at a campus event), and keep it under the word limit with room to spare.

## The selection process

Expect some combination of [online tests](/blog/psychometric-test-tips), a [video interview](/blog/async-video-interview-tips) (usually recorded questions on motivation and basic competencies), and sometimes a short phone or live interview. The bar is calibrated for first-years: they are checking you can structure an answer, show genuine interest, and hold composure on camera, not that you can price a bond.

For the recorded interview, three or four rehearsed-but-not-memorised answers cover most of what comes up: why this industry, why this firm, a time you worked in a team, a time you pushed through something difficult. [Practising them aloud on camera](/practice) before the real recording is the single highest-leverage hour of the whole application.

## Converting the week itself

Treat the week as a live assessment, because it is one. The people who convert:

- **Ask questions that show they were listening**, not questions that showcase preparation for its own sake.
- **Are visibly good to work with** in the group exercise: our [group exercise guide](/blog/group-exercise-interview-tips) applies in full.
- **Follow up properly**: a short, specific thank-you to the people they actually worked with, and a connection request with a sentence attached.
- **Say yes to the conversion interview on the spot** and treat it as a real interview, prepared the night before, not an informal chat.

## If you missed the window

No spring week does not mean no career. Plenty of interns and graduates come through open summer internship applications, industrial placements or direct graduate entry. What the spring week route mostly buys is an earlier, slightly wider door. If you are reading this as a second-year or finalist, put the same energy into [summer internship and graduate scheme applications](/blog/graduate-scheme-interview-tips): the assessment skills are identical, and the timeline is the same lesson: apply early, prepare the interview before you need it.

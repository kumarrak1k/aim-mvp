Audit and accountancy training contracts (the structured ACA, ACCA or CA routes at large and mid-tier firms) recruit continuously and at scale, and their interviews are among the most standardised in graduate hiring. That standardisation is good news: the questions are predictable, the criteria are published, and preparation moves outcomes more than brilliance does. (Investment banking and finance analyst roles run different processes: guides [here](/blog/investment-banking-interview-tips) and [here](/blog/finance-analyst-interview-tips).)

## What the process looks like

Typically: application, [online tests](/blog/psychometric-test-tips) (numerical and situational judgement), a recorded [video interview](/blog/async-video-interview-tips), then an assessment day combining a [case study](/blog/assessment-centre-case-study-tips) or written exercise, a [group task](/blog/group-exercise-interview-tips) and a final interview, often with a partner or director.

You do not need an accounting degree, and interviewers know most candidates cannot yet debit and credit. What they test instead: motivation that survives probing, professional judgement, comfort with numbers, and the client-facing behaviours audit actually runs on.

## The questions that decide it

**Why audit (or tax, or advisory), and why this firm?** The single most heavily weighted question, and the one generic answers die on. Good answers show you know what the job is: weeks spent at client sites or in data rooms, checking evidence, asking awkward questions politely, busy season and study leave in the same year. Reference the qualification route explicitly (the ACA is a three-year professional education the firm pays for) and name real reasons for the firm: sector strength, client base, an office you have actually considered living near, people you have spoken to.

**Integrity and judgement scenarios.** Audit exists because trust needs checking, so expect some version of: a client pressures you near a deadline, a number does not look right, a senior tells you to let something go. The credited pattern is consistent: do not sign what you have not verified, raise it with the person first where appropriate, escalate through the team when the issue stands, and never sit on it. Firms would rather hire a trainee who escalates too readily than one who defers too easily.

**Commercial awareness.** Not markets trivia: understanding that clients are businesses and audits are commercial engagements. Follow a couple of business stories for the fortnight before ([method here](/blog/commercial-awareness-interview-guide)), and be ready for what challenges do businesses like our clients face right now. Naming audit-adjacent pressures (cost inflation, going-concern uncertainty, regulation) shows you have thought about the work itself.

**The competency set.** Teamwork, deadlines, attention to detail, [handling pressure](/blog/tell-me-about-a-time-you-worked-under-pressure), in [STAR form](/blog/how-to-use-the-star-method). Attention to detail deserves a genuine story: a discrepancy you caught, a process you checked properly. It is the audit competency, and vague claims about being detail-oriented score nothing.

## The partner interview

The final interview is longer, warmer and more probing: your motivation retested in depth, your understanding of the firm, your questions for them. Partners are selecting colleagues for client rooms, so the informal criterion is simple: would I put this person in front of a client in eighteen months? Composure, plain speaking and honest answers about weaknesses ([done properly](/blog/how-to-answer-what-is-your-greatest-weakness)) matter more here than polish.

Have real [questions ready](/blog/questions-to-ask-at-the-end-of-an-interview): about their clients, the first-year experience, how study leave works in practice. And expect why not one of our competitors, which is just the firm question wearing armour.

## Preparation that pays

The pipeline rewards spoken fluency on a small set of questions: why audit, why us, the integrity scenario, four competency stories. Rehearse them aloud until they are conversational rather than recited: [scored practice interviews](/practice) will tell you which answers ramble and which land, and the assessment day's written and group stages are exactly what our [mock assessment centre](/assessment-centre) rehearses.

Audit hiring wants sensible, honest, numerate people who can grow in front of clients. Show up as one, with evidence, and the process is yours to lose.

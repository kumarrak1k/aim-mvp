January is the heaviest interview month of the year. Graduate schemes run their first big [assessment centre](/blog/what-to-expect-at-an-assessment-centre) waves, hiring budgets reset and paused processes restart, and the new year job-search surge means recruiters come back to full inboxes and full diaries.

Which makes the Christmas fortnight quietly valuable: a stretch of genuinely free time, two weeks before the busiest month, that most competing candidates will spend entirely on the sofa. You do not need to sacrifice your holiday. You need about five focused hours, placed well.

## First: protect the actual holiday

A candidate who rests over Christmas and prepares lightly beats one who grinds all fortnight and arrives in January stale. Rest is preparation: interviews and assessment days are energy sports. Plan the five hours, do them properly, and take the rest of the time off with a clear conscience.

## Hour one: know your January

List what is actually booked or likely: interviews scheduled, applications awaiting outcomes, centres that might invite you at two weeks' notice. Read the invitation emails properly (exercise lists, formats, platforms). This hour converts vague January anxiety into a short list of concrete events, and everything else in the plan follows from it.

If nothing is booked yet, this hour becomes application hour: several schemes and plenty of experienced-hire roles still take applications over the break ([timeline guide](/blog/graduate-scheme-interview-tips)), and applications submitted in late December meet January's fresh-budget attention.

## Hours two and three: rebuild your stories, out loud

Whatever January holds, competency questions are in it. Refresh your [story bank](/blog/competency-interview-story-bank): six to eight examples in [STAR shape](/blog/how-to-use-the-star-method), updated with anything the last term or quarter added.

Then, and this is the part that matters, speak them. Once through each story aloud, ideally to a camera or in a [scored practice session](/practice), because the winter break is long enough for spoken fluency to rust. Two hours of speaking beats ten hours of rereading notes, and it is the difference January panels can hear.

## Hour four: the specific event, rehearsed

For each January event, one targeted rehearsal: a timed [case study](/blog/assessment-centre-case-study-tips) if a centre includes one, a [presentation](/blog/presentation-interview-tips) run aloud, a [video interview](/blog/how-to-ace-a-video-interview) setup check. If your centre is virtual, do the [tech rehearsal](/blog/virtual-assessment-centre-tips) now, while the household wifi negotiation can happen calmly.

A full [mock assessment centre](/assessment-centre) fits neatly in this hour if a real one is on your January calendar: it is the closest thing to the day itself, and doing it in December means the format holds no surprises when the invitation lands.

## Hour five: the boring layer that saves you

Update the CV and have it reviewed against our [format guide](/blog/how-to-write-a-cv-uk). Check your interview clothes still fit and are clean. Confirm travel plans for early January dates (engineering works love the new year). Reread your own application for each live process, because January interviewers will quote it back at you and December-you remembers it better than January-you will.

## The night-before rule, extended

The final piece is calendar hygiene: put a fifteen-minute review the evening before each January event ([checklist here](/blog/night-before-interview-checklist)), so nothing depends on morning-of memory.

Then close the laptop and enjoy the break. You have done the five hours; the sofa is earned. January's interviews will be full of candidates who are either rusty or exhausted. You will be neither, and on such small margins offers are decided.

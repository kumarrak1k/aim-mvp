New year, new job is the most acted-upon resolution in working life: January reliably brings the year's biggest surge in applications, and recruiters return to reset hiring budgets and live requisitions. The surge is real opportunity and real competition in the same envelope, and the difference between the candidates who convert it and the ones refreshing job boards in March is rarely talent. It is structure.

Here is a plan for the first quarter, built to fit around a job or final-year workload.

## Week one: aim before you fire

The commonest January mistake is applying immediately, everywhere, with the old CV. A day of aiming beats a week of spraying:

- **Define the target**: two or three role types, sectors, salary floor, location and hybrid requirements. Written down, so scattergun temptation has something to argue with.
- **Rebuild the CV against the target** ([UK format guide](/blog/how-to-write-a-cv-uk)), and refresh [LinkedIn](/blog/linkedin-for-job-seekers) to match: recruiters search it all January.
- **Draft one strong cover letter skeleton** ([guide](/blog/how-to-write-a-cover-letter-for-a-job)) you can tailor in twenty minutes rather than write from scratch each time.

## Weeks two to six: applications with a pulse

Volume matters, but tailored volume: five to eight genuinely tailored applications a week beats thirty clones, because sifts are built to catch clones. For each: adjust the CV's top third, tailor the letter's middle paragraph, and note where the role came from and what you claimed (interviews will quote your application back at you).

Work the unadvertised layer too: tell your network you are looking (most roles filled in Q1 involve a referral somewhere), and go direct to target companies' careers pages, which post before the job boards do.

Track everything in one sheet: role, date, status, next action. Momentum in a job search is psychological, and a visible pipeline beats a vague sense of waiting.

## Prepare interviews before they exist

The January candidate's classic stumble: weeks of application effort, then a Tuesday invitation for a Thursday interview and nothing prepared. Interview readiness is buildable in advance and portable across employers:

- A [story bank](/blog/competency-interview-story-bank) of six to eight examples in [STAR shape](/blog/how-to-use-the-star-method).
- Fluent answers to the fixtures: [tell me about yourself](/blog/how-to-answer-tell-me-about-yourself), [why this job](/blog/how-to-answer-why-do-you-want-this-job), [weaknesses](/blog/how-to-answer-what-is-your-greatest-weakness), and if you are moving jobs, [why you are leaving](/blog/why-are-you-leaving-your-current-job), which must be clean and complaint-free.
- Spoken rehearsal, not silent reading: [practice interviews with scored feedback](/practice) once or twice a week through the quarter keep you match-fit, so an invitation triggers targeted research ([company method here](/blog/how-to-research-a-company-before-an-interview)) rather than panic.

When invitations land, prepare each properly: format, interviewers looked up, your application reread, and the [night-before checklist](/blog/night-before-interview-checklist) run.

## Keep the engine running

**Rejections are throughput.** Even strong candidates convert a minority of applications; a no with [feedback requested](/blog/how-to-get-interview-feedback) is a free coaching session. Adjust based on where you stall: no interviews means the CV or targeting; interviews but no offers means rehearsal and delivery.

**Guard against the February fade.** Cap the search at sensible weekly hours, take one full day off it, and put the sheet review in your calendar like a meeting. A sustainable eight weeks beats a heroic three.

**Finish properly.** When offers come, [negotiate](/blog/how-to-negotiate-a-job-offer) (January hiring managers have fresh budgets and deadlines of their own) and handle [references and the offer stage](/blog/references-and-offer-stage) without letting the momentum drop at the last fence.

Run this structure and by April you will either be placed or genuinely close, with a pipeline, a match-fit interview game and a targeting thesis refined by data. That is what a resolution looks like when it has a plan attached. Start with the aiming day: everything else follows from it.
